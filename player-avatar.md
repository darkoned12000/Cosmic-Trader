# Player Avatar System — Design & Implementation Plan

**Status:** Art direction **settled** (procedural). Phases 1–4 and 6 complete. Uploads (phase 5)
deferred by choice. Nothing else outstanding.
**Scope:** Give each pilot a visual identity chosen during account creation, persist it on the
`Player`, show it on Ship Status, and let the player customise it afterwards.
**Related docs:** `planets.md` (same doc-driven pattern), `AGENTS.md` (architecture, commands,
avatar file inventory, A3 faction colour language)

---

## 1. Goal

An account is a username, faction, and ship. This system adds a **portrait** representing the
pilot, with:

1. A species per playable faction (**Duran**, **Vinari**, **Trader**) and a **presentation**
   (male / female / neutral) — visual presentation styles only; species biology and gender are not
   otherwise defined by lore.
2. A quick selector during account creation, with a good default, skippable, and not a fourth step.
3. A **customisation layer** afterwards, so a pilot can adjust an axis at a time rather than
   re-rolling everything.
4. The portrait rendered prominently on Ship Status.

---

## 2. Art direction — **procedural generation** (settled, supersedes the original "commission art" constraint)

**Decision: portraits are generated by a deterministic `CustomPainter`. There is no portrait art
pipeline.**

This reverses the plan's original premise. That premise was *"the repo ships zero character art, so
portrait assets need to be created, commissioned, or licensed, and drawing plausible character art
procedurally is not a free substitute."* That reasoning was about **cost**, and the cost turned out
not to be payable. The trade now accepted is **quality ceiling in exchange for combinatorial
variety and zero asset maintenance**.

### Why this won

| Consideration | Commissioned art | Procedural |
|---|---|---|
| Look count | Fixed at asset count (27) | 14 persisted axes; millions of combinations |
| Player ownership | Pick 1 of N | Tune 1 of N per axis, or re-roll |
| Rights/licensing | Must be cleared per asset | Not applicable — nothing is licensed |
| Asset maintenance | Every new option needs new art + pubspec entry + inventory test | A new colour entry in one palette list |
| Consistency | Needs art direction across a whole set | Structural — one painter enforces it |
| Web/packaging | Ship megabytes of images | None |
| Plateau | Fixed by the artist | Bounded by painter quality, and *revisable in a diff* |
| Ship Status thumbnails | Asset must read at 48px | Draws at any size; tuned for 48px and 128px |

The decisive factor is the last two rows. A painter's ceiling is visible in one file and can be
raised incrementally; an art pack's quality is a property of the whole set and cannot be improved
without re-commissioning. For a game that is still pre-release, investing in the pipeline you can
still change is worth more than the pipeline you cannot.

### What we gave up, and the honest quality bar

Procedural portraiture is **not automatically good**. It fails when silhouettes are
indistinguishable, when combinations are unconstrained, and when detail turns to mush at thumbnail
size. Every one of those has been hit and fixed during this work:

- *Indistinguishable presentations* — all three Terran presentations originally shared one rounded
  skull and thin high brows, so the male faces read girlish. Fixed with presentation-specific
  skulls, brow shelves, heavier lids. **Lesson: gender is silhouette, not colour.**
- *A species that read as a recoloured human* — the Vinari originally had a humanoid skull with
  ears, nose, and a violet-tinted mouth. Fixed by removing all three. **Lesson: lore beats taste
  when they disagree.**
- *Detail that dies at 48px* — full-width armour grooves read as a smiling mouth. Fixed with fewer,
  larger, higher-contrast shapes. **Lesson: verify at the smallest size the game actually uses.**
- *Faction colour leaking into skin* — an early build tinted skin by faction colour, producing green
  Traders and red Duran. **Lesson: faction identity belongs on the ring and backdrop only.**

**The bar is: a pilot must be recognisable as their species at 48px, as their presentation at 128px,
and pleasant to look at full-size in the gallery.** Verified by rendering contact sheets and looking
at them — never by reading the code. That workflow caught a regression that every existing test
passed (see §6.4).

### Explicitly out of scope now

- Commissioning, licensing, or sourcing portrait art.
- The asset-naming convention, `pubspec.yaml` asset directory, and asset-inventory test the original
  plan called for. There are no assets to inventory.
- Modulated by size rather than pixels: there is no `cacheWidth` concern.

## 3. Lore fidelity

Each faction's existing `appearance` string is the authoritative art direction, not a suggestion.

| Faction | `appearance` | What it forces |
|---|---|---|
| `duran` | *"Scaly, insectoid warriors with glowing red eyes, clad in battle-armour"* | Long low reptilian skull; real overlapping **scales** as geometry, not a painted texture; slit pupils; segmented plate armour, never fabric. **Gender is silhouette only** — crest rake/count, horn lean, plating density. A per-presentation palette would be wrong: *"girl colours just don't make sense in a warrior race."* |
| `vinari` | *"Glowing, fluid forms with shifting colors, like living auroras"* | Not a purple human. No ears, no nose, no mouth, no sclera. Elongated tapering form, internal luminous core, drift motes. |
| `trader` | *"Diverse Terrans in practical jumpsuits or merchant finery"* | Human anatomy — ears, nose, mouth, sclera. Variety is carried by **kit**, which is what the lore actually describes. |

`pirate` exists in the enum but is **excluded from registration** (also excluded from
`Faction.allFactions()`), so it gets no species. See §10 for a later reuse.

**Colour ownership is the rule most easily broken.** `AvatarPalette` owns skin, garment, eye, hair,
and marking pigment per species. `FactionPalette` owns the ring, the backdrop bloom, and insignia —
nothing else. `test/avatar_catalog_test.dart` asserts the two never collide and that the lore colour
bands hold (Terrans warm, Duran never red, Vinari always bluish). Do not "simplify" by deriving one
from the other.

## 4. Data model — `lib/data/models/avatar_selection.dart`

Persist **stable seed IDs**, not list positions or user-visible labels. Reordering the catalogue must
not change a player's saved portrait.

```dart
enum AvatarSpecies { duran, vinari, terran }        // one per playable faction
enum AvatarPresentation { male, female, neutral }   // visual style, not biological lore
enum AvatarSource { preset, custom }                // `custom` reserved; see §8

@immutable
class AvatarSelection {
  final int schemaVersion;
  final AvatarSpecies species;              // derived from the player's faction
  final AvatarPresentation presentation;    // displayed as Male / Female / Neutral
  final String portraitId;                  // stable key, e.g. duran_male_02
  final AvatarSource source;
  final String? customFile;                 // unused while uploads are deferred
  final AvatarStyle? style;                 // null = use the catalogue's seeded look
}
```

Contract, all of it implemented and tested:

- `fromJson` **never throws**: unknown, legacy, or missing keys → a valid faction default.
- `==`/`hashCode` implemented, and they **include `style`** — otherwise a style-only change in the
  editor compares equal and is never saved.
- Round-trip: `AvatarSelection.fromJson(selection.toJson()) == selection`.
- Unknown enum names and removed portrait IDs resolve to a faction default rather than crashing a
  login or showing a missing-asset box.
- `forFaction(faction)` repairs species/portrait mismatches. `Player` stores faction separately, so
  the two can drift.
- `Player.avatar` is a **nullable** field with an `effectiveAvatar` getter. Migration is therefore
  inert — no backfill pass over `players.json` is needed and pre-avatar saves render unchanged.

### 4.1 `AvatarStyle` — 14 axes, the actual customisation surface

The catalogue's 27 entries (3 species × 3 presentations × 3) are **seeds**, not finished portraits.
The style is what makes a pilot individual. `null` for the whole style means "use the seed's look".

**Shape axes** — every one is 3 options (`tone` is 6):

| Axis | Options | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `tone` | 6 | Species skin tone |
| `outfit` | 3 | Species garment set (Duran armour / Vinari shroud / Terran suit-coat-rig) |
| `outfitTone` | 3 | Garment base colour |
| `hair` | 3 | Terran hairstyle, Duran chitin crest, Vinari light-drift — **species-specific** |
| `horns` | 3 | Duran only: forward-curved / tall crown / short barbel |
| `eyes` | 3 | Eye shape: round / narrow / angular |
| `markings` | 0–2 | None / war paint. **Duran + Trader only**; 0 is "unmarked" |
| `expression` | 3 | Neutral / stern / faintly amused |

**Decoupled colour slots** — all nullable, 3 options each:

| Slot | Controls |
|---|---|
| `hairColor` | Hair / crest / drift colour |
| `eyeColor` | Iris colour |
| `markingColor` | Marking pigment |
| `trimColor` | Garment trim and accent |
| `gear` | Worn headgear: none / comms headset / goggles |
| `backdrop` | Environment: Duran forge glow / ember field / void, Vinari aurora / nebula / deep drift, Terran hangar / starfield / viewport |

`gear` and `backdrop` are not colours, but they are nullable slots with the same
null-means-legacy semantics, so they behave identically. `gear` additionally has a
*coupling* to resolve (`effectiveGear`, below); `backdrop` has none, because the original
backdrop was not tied to any other axis.

### 4.2 `null` means **coupled**, and that is the compatibility mechanism

Before the colour slots existed, `hair` chose a silhouette *and* its colour together, `eyes` chose a
shape *and* an iris, `markings` chose a pattern *and* a pigment. Three options gave three looks
instead of nine. The slots are nullable precisely so that **a null slot reproduces the old
shape-keyed behaviour exactly**:

| Slot | Resolver | Legacy fallback |
|---|---|---|
| `hairColor` | `AvatarPalette.hair(species, shape, dye)` | `legacyHair(species, shape)` |
| `eyeColor` | `AvatarPalette.eye(species, shape, dye)` | `legacyEye(species, shape)` |
| `markingColor` | `AvatarPalette.markingPigment(species, pattern, dye)` | `legacyMarkingPigment(species, pattern)` |
| `trimColor` | `AvatarPalette.trim(species, outfitTone, dye)` | `outfitTrim(species, outfitTone)` |
| `gear` | `AvatarStyle.effectiveGear` | `outfit` coupling: 1 → headset, 2 → goggles, else none |
| `backdrop` | `SpeciesArt.backdrop` dispatching to `backdropVariant` | `SpeciesArt.factionBloom` — the single faction bloom every portrait used before the axis existed |

`AxisStyleAxis` — the stable identity of each axis — lives in this file rather than beside the
editor UI, because `AvatarStyle.rerolled` has to honour per-axis locks and the data layer must not
import a widget library. It is also what per-axis UI state keys off, so the lock toggle does not
depend on a widget instance surviving a rebuild.

Consequences, all deliberate:

- A pre-existing save renders **byte-identical**. `test/avatar_selection_test.dart` asserts each
  resolver's null branch equals the list it replaced, for every species and shape.
- A fully coupled style writes **no new JSON keys**, so its payload is unchanged.
- `AvatarStyle.isCoupled` reports whether anything is overridden.
- **Out-of-range colour values coerce to `null` rather than clamping.** Null means "fall back to the
  shape's own colour", which is a valid look; clamping would silently snap a corrupt save to an
  arbitrary end of a list.
- `copyWith` uses explicit `clearHairColor` / `clearEyeColor` / … flags, because `?? this.x` cannot
  clear a nullable slot. Same precedent as `clearCustomFile` on `AvatarSelection`.
- Re-rolls leave each slot `null` roughly one roll in four, so coupled looks stay reachable without a
  separate reset affordance.

`AvatarStyle.reroll` re-rolls every axis, including drawing a new catalogue seed. It **subsumes** the
original plan's "Random pick" — that separate action was removed as redundant. It is no longer the
only control: §6 adds per-axis editing, per-axis reset, and per-axis locks.

### 4.3 Determinism

Seeds derive from the stable portrait ID via FNV-1a. All seed arithmetic stays inside 2^53 so web and
native produce identical portraits. `String.hashCode` is never used — it is not a persistence
contract. `test/avatar_catalog_test.dart` pins the 32-bit / 2^53 bound.

## 5. Rendering — `lib/widgets/avatar/`

### `avatar_catalog.dart`

The portrait registry: stable IDs → species, presentation, draw seed, seeded style, default status.
Deliberately **free of any `package:flutter` import** so `data/models` can depend on it. The
`avatar_selection.dart` ↔ `avatar_catalog.dart` import cycle is legal Dart and kept harmless by that
rule — **do not add a widget import to the catalogue, or the data layer starts pulling in Flutter.**

### `avatar_palette.dart`

Per-species skin, garment, eye, hair, and marking-pigment palettes, plus the decoupled dye lists and
the legacy fallbacks from §4.2. Separate from `factionColor()` on purpose (§3).

### `avatar_species_art.dart` — the seam

`AvatarDrawContext` carries everything a renderer needs for one portrait, so the species files take
one argument instead of a fifteen-parameter call, and a new colour is added in exactly two places
rather than threaded through every signature.

`abstract class SpeciesArt` declares four required slots — `appendages`, `headPath`, `faceTexture`,
`outfit` — plus `crest` and overridable shared drawing (`headShading`, `eyes`, `expression`,
`lidWidth`).

**Layer order is load-bearing and documented on the class:**

1. backdrop
2. `outfit` — torso and garment
3. `appendages` — anything past the skull (horns, frills, ears, gear), **before the head fill**
4. `headPath` fill
5. `headShading` + `faceTexture` — inside `clipPath(headPath)`
6. `eyes`, `expression`, `crest`

Step 3 before step 4 is the fix for a real bug: appendages used to be drawn *inside* the head clip,
which sliced the Duran horn roots flat and lost the Terran ears almost entirely. A new species cannot
accidentally repeat that.

**Coordinate contract:** `headCenter`, `headR`, and `shoulderTop` on the context are **pixels**.
`u()` / `ux()` / `uy()` take **fractions**. Wrapping an already-pixel value in `uy()` double-scales
it. That mistake shipped once — silently, with every garment pushed off-canvas — and is now guarded
by a pixel-sampling test (§6.4).

### The three species files

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `avatar_duran_art.dart` | Scaled skull, overlapping scales as geometry, slit pupils, chitin crest, presentation-bent horns, three armour sets |
| `avatar_vinari_art.dart` | Non-humanoid luminous form, drift motes, three shroud sets |
| `avatar_terran_art.dart` | Presentation-specific skulls, heavier male brow and lid, ears, gear, zip / buttons / harness |

### `avatar_portrait_painter.dart`

**Orchestration only** — layer order, palette lookup, context build, backdrop. This is the one file
to change when the drawing model itself changes.

### `avatar_canvas.dart`

Size-clipped renderer with the faction ring, a selected-halo variant, semantic labels, and a total
fallback to the species default.

### `avatar_gallery.dart`

Reusable selector for registration and editing: presentation tabs, large live preview, thumbnail
strip, a faction flavour line from `Faction.forClass(fc).appearance`, and the single Reroll action.
Tapping a portrait tile adopts that seed's look with `clearStyle: true`, so the tile is honest about
what it shows.

### `../screens/avatar_editor_screen.dart`

`Scaffold` + Save/Cancel draft wrapper around a **pinned** preview plus `AvatarDesigner`. The preview
is deliberately outside the scroll view — see §6.2. Nothing is written to the player until Save
succeeds.

## 6. The customisation layer — **complete**

The gallery is a flat list plus one Reroll that changes everything. That is fine for a first
impression and poor for a pilot who likes everything about their portrait except their hairstyle.
Three additions, all shipped.

### 6.1 Backdrop axis — **done**

`AvatarStyle.backdrop: int?`, nullable with the same null-means-legacy rule. Per-species options,
drawn from the plan's original layer catalogue:

| Species | Options |
|---|---|
| Duran | forge glow · ember field · void |
| Vinari | aurora · nebula · deep drift |
| Terran | hangar · starfield · viewport |

The axis is a slot on the `SpeciesArt` seam, not painter code, so the three species own their own
environments the same way they own their own anatomy. `SpeciesArt.backdrop` dispatches: `null` →
`factionBloom`, set → `backdropVariant(c, index)`, which defaults back to `factionBloom` so a
species with no bespoke options — or an index added without art to match — still renders something
rather than nothing.

Rules, all of which held on the first render pass:

- The **ring stays faction-coloured** regardless of backdrop. Backdrop is environment; the ring is
  identity.
- A backdrop is decoration, not a species cue, and may not be the only thing distinguishing two
  portraits.
- `null` renders the original bloom, byte-identical.
- Backdrops must read at 48px. A backdrop that only works at 128px is noise in the thumbnail strip.

**Two traps this axis walked into, both caught by rendering:**

- *The viewport competed with the pilot.* The first planet limb was a bright teal
  (`#2E6B8A`) and became the most luminous thing in frame, pulling the eye off the face — a
  direct violation of the rule above. Dulled to `#1B3A49` and the atmosphere line halved. The face
  is now unambiguously the brightest thing in every backdrop.
- *Bright stars clumped into a lens-flare column.* Five bright stars with a wide halo read as one
  artefact whenever the draw happened to place them in a loose vertical line. Tightened the halo
  from `0.030` to `0.015`.

**A compatibility trap worth naming, because it is not obvious from the code.** The backdrop is
drawn at step 1, *before* the pilot, and `AvatarDrawContext.rng` is a single shared sequence whose
draw order is effectively part of the save format — the Vinari drift motes and the Terran hair lean
consume from it. Taking random draws from `rng` inside a backdrop would shift every later consumer
and silently repaint every portrait saved before this axis existed: the exact failure the
null-means-legacy rule exists to prevent. `AvatarDrawContext.scoped(key)` derives an independent
generator from the draw seed, and any layer drawn before the species slots must use it rather than
`rng`.

### 6.2 Tabbed editor — **done**

`avatar_designer.dart` restructures the editor into categories, because 14 axes in one flat list is
unreadable. Tabs follow the axes' own groupings, not an arbitrary split:

| Tab | Axes |
|---|---|
| Portrait | *(not axes)* seed picker, presentation, reroll — reuses `AvatarGallery` |
| Face | `tone`, `eyes`, `eyeColor`, `markings`, `markingColor`, `expression` |
| Hair | `hair`, `hairColor`, `horns` |
| Gear | `gear` |
| Outfit | `outfit`, `outfitTone`, `trimColor` |
| Backdrop | `backdrop` |

The **Portrait** tab is an addition to the table above. The seed strip and presentation picker are
not axes but have to live somewhere, and reusing `AvatarGallery` (with `showPreview: false`) keeps
registration and editing in step instead of duplicating the picker.

All axis metadata — labels, swatches, applicability — is declared in `avatar_axis_catalog.dart`, so
adding an axis is one entry there rather than a new block of UI. Two rules it exists to enforce:

- **No axis is a row of integers.** Every option is a named choice: "Angular", "Crown spikes",
  "Forge glow". Colour options carry a swatch *and* a name; `Auto` gets a glyph rather than an
  empty swatch that would read as a rendering fault.
- **An axis that does nothing for the species is hidden, not shown inert.** `horns` is Duran-only
  and the Vinari are unmarked by lore. A control that silently does nothing is worse than none,
  because the player reasonably assumes they changed something. `optionsFor` returns empty for an
  inapplicable axis rather than inventing a name for it.

#### Three defects the build surfaced

1. **A crash that emptied the Face tab.** Option lists were generated by looping to
   `AvatarStyle.toneCount` (6) for axes whose real source has 3 entries — the skin list has 6, but
   every dye list and the garment list have 3. That threw a `RangeError` *while building* the row,
   so the tab rendered one axis and then nothing. Counts are now derived from the list that
   actually supplies them, and `test/avatar_axis_catalog_test.dart` pins every axis's count against
   its source.
2. **Reset did not reset.** `AvatarStyle.copyWith` resolves `?? this.x`, so writing a `null` colour
   kept the old value — meaning "return this axis to its seed" silently did nothing for any axis
   whose seed is `null`, which is every colour slot. The nullable-slot writers now use the explicit
   `clear*` flags. Caught by a test that resets a style differing from the seed on *every* axis.
3. **The pinned preview was not pinned.** The first version put the preview, the designer, and the
   buttons in one `SingleChildScrollView`. It looked fine and meant changing a hairstyle happened
   off-screen — the precise failure the tabbed editor exists to prevent. The layout is now a pinned
   preview, an `Expanded` designer, and a pinned button row, with only the axis list scrolling.

#### Layout decisions worth keeping

- **The preview is responsive**: `(height * 0.21).clamp(84, 132)`, so a short viewport shrinks the
  preview rather than squeezing the axis list out of existence.
- **Tabs are text-only.** With an icon stacked above each label, six tabs overflowed a 430px phone
  and `Backdrop` sat off-screen with no affordance that the strip scrolled — a tap on it silently
  did nothing. Text-only plus `labelPadding: 7` fits all six at 430px with ~44px to spare, and
  degrades to normal scrolling at 360px.
- **The gallery gained `showPreview`.** The editor already pins a larger preview, so showing the
  gallery's own pushed the seed strip below the fold.
- The gallery's thumbnail strip height is now **measured** with a `TextPainter` instead of
  `thumbnailSize + spacing(30)`, which overflowed by a pixel at sizes the magic number did not
  anticipate. Relatedly, `_ActionTile` hard-coded a 64px circle regardless of the strip size —
  harmless at the default and a real overflow the moment a caller passed something smaller.

### 6.3 Per-axis locks — **done**

**This is the mitigation for the combinatorics risk, and the reason the parameter designer is safe.**

14 axes produce far more combinations than anyone has visually reviewed, so exposing every axis
unconstrained means players land on combinations nobody checked. A lock lets a player declare "these
axes are settled" and have a re-roll respect that.

- A lock is a **transient editor concern, not persisted state.** It describes how someone wants to
  re-roll, not what their pilot is; it is spent the moment they Save. Storing it would add schema for
  a decision already made. `test/avatar_selection_test.dart` asserts the style's JSON stays a flat set
  of ints with no lock key, so this cannot drift.
- **Locks start empty.** A surprising number of players should find Reroll still means "surprise me".
- **Per axis, not per tab.** A player may lock their outfit while re-rolling hair. The count on the
  re-roll button is global, because a re-roll is.
- **A locked axis keeps its current value, including a `null` colour slot.** Locking is therefore
  orthogonal to the null-means-coupled rule: locking a still-coupled dye locks the coupled value, and
  unlocking it lets the next roll decouple it again.
- **Lock and Reset are independent.** A lock governs re-rolling, a reset governs the value. Resetting
  a locked axis does not unlock it, and locking a modified axis does not hide the "changed" marker.
- **The re-roll button stops lying.** With locks it reads "Reroll unlocked" rather than "Reroll
  everything", with the count in the tooltip. The count is deliberately *not* in the label: "Reroll
  unlocked (3)" wrapped to two lines on a 430px phone and made that button taller than its neighbour.

`AxisStyleAxis` lives in the **model**, not beside the editor UI, because `AvatarStyle.rerolled` has
to honour locks and the data layer must not import a widget library.

`AvatarStyle.reroll` became a thin wrapper over a new instance method, `rerolled(nextInt, {locked})`.
That method rolls from the **current** style rather than from neutral, because a lock has nothing to
hold on to otherwise — with no locks the two are indistinguishable. `AvatarSelection.randomized`
gained the same `locked` parameter, and the gallery's own Reroll tile takes a `locked` set, so the two
re-roll entry points cannot disagree about what a re-roll does.

### 6.4 Verification — render, don't read

Every visual change in this system is verified by **baking PNG contact sheets and looking at them**,
at both 48px and 128px. Reading the code does not work: it missed a green-Traders/red-Duran bug and a
glowing-mouth armour seam that were obvious on screen.

The workflow is a throwaway `test/zz_render_test.dart` using `ui.PictureRecorder` + `Canvas` (not
`toImage` on a widget tree, which hangs), then `rm` it. Delete the harness before finishing.

**Exception-only tests are not sufficient here**, and neither are tests that cannot fail. The
`c.uy()` double-scaling regression drew every Duran armour, Vinari shroud, and Terran collar
entirely off-canvas, threw nothing, and passed all 380 tests. So
`test/avatar_outfit_render_test.dart` samples actual rendered pixels. Each guard in it was verified
by injecting the fault it exists to catch:

| Guard | Catches | Verified by |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-tone torso | Garment drawn off-canvas | Re-injecting the scale bug → *"got 1 distinct torso colours"* |
| Backdrops distinct, none is the bloom | An index falling through to the default case, or an un-art-directed option silently rendering as the bloom | Making backdrop 2 call the same art as backdrop 0 |
| Appendages outside the head clip | The original layer-order bug that sliced horn roots and lost the ears | Moving `appendages` back inside `clipPath(headPath)` |

**Widget tests have two measurement traps of their own**, both hit while building the tabbed editor:

- **The default test font is about twice as wide as a real one** — every glyph is a full-width box.
  An assertion that the six-tab strip fits a 430px phone "failed" by 90px and looked like a real
  layout bug. It was not. `test/avatar_designer_test.dart` loads the bundled `Audiowide.ttf` via
  `FontLoader` (inside `tester.runAsync`, or the load never completes) before asserting anything
  about whether text fits.
- **`setSurfaceSize` does not change `view.physicalSize`.** It still reports the binding's 800×600
  at dpr 3, so `expect(rect.right, lessThanOrEqualTo(view.physicalSize.width / dpr))` passes no
  matter how far the row overflows. Measure against a rendered widget's rect instead.

And the general form of both: **a tap on an off-screen widget silently does nothing** in
`flutter_test` — it warns and moves on. Any test that taps a tab must either assert the *content*
changed afterwards, or scroll the target into view first.

#### Never assert a random outcome

Three of the lock tests were written as "after a random re-roll, this axis should differ from its
seed", which is true 3 times in 4 for a 3-option axis. The fix is a **forced generator**:
`int nextInt(int max) => max - 1` against a base whose every axis is `0`. Now any axis that gets
rolled is *guaranteed* to differ, so "unlocked axes must move" is deterministic, and "locking every
axis is a no-op" is too. The complementary half — that a locked axis *held* — is deterministic by
construction, and is what the widget tests assert, since the editor's own re-roll draws from
`math.Random()` and cannot be forced from outside.

**`pumpWidget` with an identical widget tree reuses the existing `State`.** A test asserting "locks
do not survive reopening" therefore has to genuinely tear the editor down — pump something else in
between — or it re-reads the very locks it is claiming to have discarded.

**A guard that cannot fail is worse than no guard**, because it reports safety it has not verified.
One was written and deleted: "a backdrop leaves the pilot unchanged". It compared the face across
backdrops, and it passed two deliberate faults — including a translucent green wash over the entire
box — because the head fill is opaque and drawn *after* the backdrop, so anything the backdrop
draws is covered regardless of what it contains. It was structurally incapable of failing. The
appendage guard replaced it, since that layer-order mistake is one that *can* actually happen.

That file is deliberately standalone: `Picture.toImage` never completes inside the fake-async zone
`testWidgets` runs in, and it hangs if mixed with widget tests in the same file.

## 7. Integration

### 7.1 Registration — still three stages

| Step | Builder | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `_buildCredentialsStep()` | unchanged |
| 1 | `_buildFactionStep()` + portrait gallery | faction selection and a matching portrait preview |
| 2 | `_buildShipStep()` | unchanged; ship selection and LAUNCH |

- The step indicator remains three stages. Do not add a fourth.
- The choice is **skippable**: a recommended portrait is preselected and Continue works without
  opening any customisation surface.
- Selecting a faction updates the available portraits, selects a valid faction default, and resets
  the ship as before.
- `_register()` passes the `AvatarSelection` to `PlayerStorage.register()`.
- Registration uses the **compact gallery**, not the tabbed editor.

### 7.2 `Player` and `PlayerStorage`

Five coordinated `player.dart` edits (field, constructor default, `copyWith`, `toJson`, `fromJson`)
following the existing tolerant pattern:

```dart
avatar: json['avatar'] is Map
    ? AvatarSelection.fromJson((json['avatar'] as Map).cast<String, dynamic>())
    : null,   // resolved by effectiveAvatar
```

- `Player.avatar` is nullable; `Player.effectiveAvatar` is the single consistently named getter.
  Migration stays inert — no backfill over `players.json`.
- `copyWith` must distinguish "leave unchanged" from "clear".
- `PlayerStorage.register(...)` takes an `AvatarSelection` and assigns a valid faction default when
  none is supplied.
- `savePlayers` **swallows write errors** (only `debugPrint`), and `updatePlayer` returns a `bool`
  some callers still ignore. The avatar Save path in `ship_status.dart` *does* check it. Making
  storage dependable is a prerequisite for any transactional work (uploads), not for the
  customisation layer.

### 7.3 Migration for existing accounts

Old records have no `avatar` key → `effectiveAvatar` resolves a valid faction default. No migration
script, no JSON backfill. A one-time "Choose your pilot portrait" hint may point at the editor, but
must be dismissible and must not block play. Avoid making a neutral/androgyne look the unexplained
default for existing accounts.

Independently, `register()` mints ids as `DateTime.now().millisecondsSinceEpoch.toString()`, which
collides in the same millisecond and makes accounts unaddressable in `updatePlayer`. `uuid` is
already a dependency; switching to `Uuid().v4()` is a contained persistence fix, not an avatar
requirement.

### 7.4 Ship Status

Keep the rocket icon in `_shipHeaderCard()` — it labels the ship-focused header, and replacing it
with a face weakens the meaning. In `_playerInfoCard()`, show `AvatarCanvas(player.effectiveAvatar)`
as a compact identity row with faction colour as a restrained border/accent, **not** a tint over the
portrait. Add a **Change Appearance** row mirroring the Change Password row (same `leading` icon size
20, same zero `contentPadding`, same chevron), opening `AvatarEditorScreen`; call `onPlayerUpdate`
only after the save succeeds.

> **Deviation from the original plan:** `_buildTwoColumnGrid` pins `mainAxisExtent: 440`, and a
> portrait plus a Change Appearance row does not fit that. The cell is now **520**. No new card was
> added, which is what the original plan actually warned against.

## 8. Uploads — **deferred**

Custom image upload (`§7` of the original plan) is **not scheduled**. Procedural generation reduces
its appeal: every pilot already has a look nobody else can produce, so the value of importing a
photo drops.

The analysis was sound and is preserved here for whenever it is picked up:

- `file_picker: ^8.1.7` is already a dependency and can return bytes with `withData: true`.
- Web has no `path_provider` file storage. Either implement a browser-backed store or **disable
  uploads on web explicitly**. Never silently accept an upload that will not survive reload.
- Validate before decode: sniff magic bytes rather than trusting extensions, cap input bytes
  (~5 MB) and decoded pixels, reject animated formats, honour EXIF orientation during
  normalization, and dispose both `ImageDescriptor` and its `ImmutableBuffer` on every path.
- Normalize to square 256×256 PNG, or preserve the verified format **and** extension. Never save
  JPEG bytes under a `.png` name.
- `playerId` does not exist at pick time. Stage bytes in memory during the draft; on account
  creation generate the UUID first, write atomically, save the player with a **bare filename only**,
  and **delete the file if the player save fails**.
- On replacement, write the new file first, save, and only then delete the old one.
- `FileSafe.writeBytes` does not exist and must preserve the existing temp/rename crash-safety.
- Requires the dependable save contract from §7.2.

`AvatarSource.custom` and `customFile` remain in the schema as reserved slots. `AvatarSource.custom`
falls back to the selected preset today.

## 11. Risks

1. **The procedural plateau.** Painter quality is a ceiling that can be raised but not escaped. The
   mitigation is that the ceiling is in one diffable place. If a future pass finds it cannot be
   raised acceptably, the migration is to an art pack — and the model, catalogue, and editor are
   already asset-agnostic enough to survive that.
2. **Combinatorics.** 14 axes produce far more combinations than anyone has visually reviewed, and
   some will be unattractive. Per-axis locks (§6.3) mitigate this by letting a player protect the
   axes they have settled, but they do not remove it: an unlocked axis can still land somewhere
   unreviewed. Enforce only authored-sane options, and do not expose unconstrained combinations.
3. **Previewing is the only review mechanism.** Nothing in the test suite can tell you a portrait
   looks good, and the same is true of the editor's *usability* — a passing widget test says the
   Face tab builds, not that it is readable. Screenshotting the editor is what found the option-list
   crash that emptied the tab, the pinned preview that was not pinned, and the tab strip whose last
   tab could not be tapped. Render-and-look is mandatory, and it has caught bugs every test passed.
4. **Coordinate contract.** Pixel values and fractional helpers in the same expression class. Already
   caused one silent, fully-passing regression; now pixel-guarded.
5. **Draw-order is part of the save format.** `AvatarDrawContext.rng` is a shared sequence, so
   adding a draw to any layer shifts every later consumer. Layers drawn before the species slots
   must use `scoped(key)`, never `rng`.
6. **Lore drift.** An axis that contradicts the `appearance` string is a bug even if it looks good.
   The Vinari-with-a-nose build is the cautionary example.
7. **Registration friction.** Customisation must stay out of account creation. If the editor ever
   feels like it belongs in registration, it is too heavy.
8. **Faction colour leaking into skin.** Has happened once. The palette test exists to keep it from
   happening again.
9. **Storage reliability.** `savePlayers` swallows errors. Fine today; a blocker for anything
   transactional.
10. **Accessibility.** Every option needs a text label and a visible selected state. Never rely on
   colour alone, and never on species alone.
11. **Defaulting.** Avoid a neutral/androgyne unexplained default for existing accounts. Defaults
    should be recognisably someone.
